TITLE: The Ground Beneath Software Engineering Is Moving

[Scene 1 — Opening]

Software engineering is changing faster than many people can emotionally process.

Not just technically.

Economically. Psychologically. Professionally.

--------------------------------------------------

[Scene 2 — Compression]

The industry once allowed room for slow growth.

Mistakes.
Mentorship.
Gradual improvement.

But now, everything feels compressed.

Faster delivery.
Faster learning.
Faster adaptation.

And many developers are quietly asking:

“How do you grow naturally in an environment that expects acceleration everywhere?”

--------------------------------------------------

[Scene 3 — Economic Fear]

The deepest fear is not really about AI itself.

It’s about becoming economically replaceable.

Developers worry that:
years of effort may lose value,
entry-level opportunities may shrink,
and AI-assisted seniors may replace multiple juniors.

Productivity is increasing.

But insecurity is increasing too.

--------------------------------------------------

[Scene 4 — The Missing Ladder]

Historically, junior engineers learned through repetition.

Small fixes.
Gradual exposure.
Time.

But AI increasingly absorbs those beginner-level tasks.

Which creates an uncomfortable question:

“How do people become experienced engineers if the learning ladder disappears?”

The industry still doesn’t fully know.

--------------------------------------------------

[Scene 5 — Fundamentals]

People often say:

“Just learn fundamentals.”

And fundamentals still matter.

But now learners are questioning something deeper:

“Is long-term investment itself still rational?”

--------------------------------------------------

[Scene 6 — Constant Acceleration]

Every day brings:
new models,
new tools,
new benchmarks,
new predictions.

Human psychology was never designed for this level of nonstop technological acceleration.

Eventually, it starts feeling like human effort itself is depreciating in real time.

--------------------------------------------------

[Scene 7 — Closing]

But complete paralysis is dangerous too.

Because while tools change rapidly,
some abilities remain less fragile:

understanding systems,
debugging complexity,
adapting continuously,
communicating clearly,
learning deeply.

Maybe this is simply a transitional era.

Not fully stable.
Not fully understood.

But honest uncertainty may be healthier than pretending everything is either perfectly fine…

or completely doomed.

--------------------------------------------------

[Final Frame]

“The ground beneath software engineering is moving.”

— And everyone is trying to find stable footing.
